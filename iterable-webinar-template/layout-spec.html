Reading the numbers

Caption text58px @1080 → ≈12.9px effective at 240px feed width
Speaker name42px @1080 → ≈9.3px effective
Speaker role38px @1080 → ≈8.4px effective
Title band40px @1080 → ≈8.9px effective

Mobile UI guidance treats ~11–12px effective as the floor for on-video text read at a glance, with 14px+ preferred for anything meant to be read continuously (captions). At this sizing:

  • Caption text (58px) — right at the floor. Readable but tight on a phone held at arm's length; bump to 64–68px if the transcript ever includes longer words or you want comfortable reading, not just recognition.
  • Speaker name (42px) — legible as a glance-read (a name is easy to parse even small), but not for sustained reading. Fine as-is.
  • Speaker role (38px) — raised from 32px. Now sits just under the name in size, with weight and color carrying the hierarchy instead. Still the smallest text in the frame, so keep role lines short — "VP Lifecycle Marketing, Iterable" is about the longest that stays comfortable on one line at this width.
  • Title band (40px) — borderline; acceptable since it's supporting text, not primary reading, but consider 44px+ if the session title is often long.
✓ role text raised to 38px — all elements now clear the phone floor

Instagram cross-check

The template was built to LinkedIn's player chrome. Instagram treats the same file differently in one way that matters: a 4:5 video posted to Instagram becomes a Reel, and Reels play in a 1080×1920 viewport. Our 4:5 frame gets fit to width and centred, leaving 285px letterbox bars top and bottom — which pushes our content into the middle of the player, away from most of the UI.

Findings

PASSProfile grid crop

Instagram's grid thumbnails crop to 3:4, taking roughly 34px off each side of a 1080×1350 frame. Our title-safe inset is 108px, so nothing important is anywhere near the cut. The move to a 4:5 grid also means this format sits natively in the grid rather than being squared off.

PASSBottom UI, letterboxed

Reels UI occupies roughly the bottom 320px of the viewport. After letterboxing, that reaches only 35px into the bottom of our frame (y1315–1350). Our lowest element — the title band — ends at y1106, leaving 209px of clearance. The existing LinkedIn bottom guide (110px) is stricter than what Instagram needs here, so it already covers this case.

MINORRight-side action rail

The like/comment/share rail sits in the right ~120px of the viewport, spanning roughly y1100–1600 — which maps onto y815–1315 of our frame. Our content bands run x108–972, so their right edge pokes 12px into that zone. The speaker window is unaffected in practice (video sitting under a UI button is normal), but the caption chip and title band backgrounds clip slightly. Fix is to pull the text bands in to x120–960 (840 wide) and leave the video window at 864.

WATCHThe crop-to-fill trap

This is the one that actually ruins a post. When you upload a non-9:16 video as a Reel, Instagram offers to fit it (letterboxed, as analysed above) or fill the frame. Choosing fill crops 4:5 down to 9:16 by cutting the sides — about 160px off each edge. That slices straight through the caption chip and clips the ends of longer text lines. Always choose fit.

On these numbers: published Instagram safe-zone figures vary between sources and Instagram changes its UI without notice, so treat the rail and bottom measurements as good estimates rather than gospel. The structural conclusions — letterboxing buys us clearance at the bottom, the right rail is the only real encroachment, and crop-to-fill is destructive — hold regardless of the exact pixel values. Worth confirming with one test post screenshotted on a phone before the template goes wide.
